# 10-minute presentation script — PLS Group (ASX:PLS)
### FMAA x Shared Value Project 2026 · title through slide 15 · appendix held for Q&A

**Rehearse to 9:00, not 10:00.** Nerves add about 10% to your pace and you will lose
20–30 seconds to handovers you did not plan for. A team that finishes at 9:05 looks
composed. A team cut off at 10:00 mid-sentence loses the close, which is the only part
the judges carry into deliberation.

**The clock below budgets 8:35 and the script measures 7:24 of actual speech at a normal
pace.** That gap is the pauses, the emphasis and the three handovers. It is not spare
time to fill. Do not spend it in advance.

---

## The single most important thing to say out loud

Slide 11 already carries it: A$0.43 against the A$6.14 target is 4.6% and looks like
rounding. Against the A$0.66 of upside it is 66% and it is the whole case.

One step further, and this is the sentence that decides whether the pitch lands:

> **Strip those operating decisions out and the target is about A$5.71. That is a 5.7%
> total return against a 5.90% benchmark. We would be recommending a hold.**

Checked: A$6.14 less A$0.43 is A$5.71, which is 4.1% above the A$5.48 close, plus a
1.6% yield. The shared-value argument is not decoration on this recommendation. It is
the reason there is one.

Say it on slide 1 or slide 11. Not both.

# SPEAKER A — the recommendation

## Title · 8s budget · 15 words (~6s spoken)

> "Good morning. We're recommending PLS Group, ASX ticker PLS, a spodumene producer in
> the Pilbara."

Do not introduce four names. Judges have your team sheet.

---

## Slide 1 — Recommendation · 50s budget · 110 words (~44s spoken)

> "Buy. Twelve-month target of six dollars fourteen against a last close of five dollars
> forty-eight. That's 12.0% of price and a 1.6% yield, so a 13.6% total return against
> a benchmark that returned 5.90%. The fund needs two points of outperformance. This
> gives seven point seven.
>
> The part that matters isn't the target. It's where the upside comes from. Sixty-six
> cents of upside, and forty-three cents of it traces to operating decisions PLS made
> for environmental reasons that the market is pricing as ordinary cost control.
>
> Strip those decisions out and we'd be recommending a hold. That's the pitch, and
> we'll spend the next eight minutes defending that forty-three cents."

**Land this:** 66% of the upside, not 4.6% of the target.

**Cut if behind:** the benchmark sentence. Keep the forty-three cents.

---

## Slide 2 — Why PLS, not the other five · 35s budget · 81 words (~32s spoken)

> "The case gave us six miners. Four of them use ESG spending to defend cash flows they
> already have. Rehabilitation provisions, emissions targets on an iron ore business
> that sells the same tonne either way. That's competent risk management. It isn't
> shared value.
>
> PLS is the only one where the societal need *is* the demand. Decarbonisation is why
> the customer exists, and carbon intensity is becoming a condition of sale. That's the
> screen, and it only selects one of the six."

**Land this:** for four of six, the tonne sells either way.

**Cut if behind:** the examples in sentence two.

> **Handover:** "So what does the company look like underneath that?"

# SPEAKER B — company and industry

## Slide 3 — FY26 results · 18s budget · 38 words (~15s spoken)

Do not read four statistics off the screen. Judges read faster than you speak.

> "One line on the year. Record volumes, a 59% margin, and unit costs *down* 9% while
> volumes rose 17%. Costs falling while volumes rise, through the worst lithium
> downturn in a decade. That's the operating position we're underwriting."

**Land this:** costs down 9%, volumes up 17%, same year.

---

## Slide 4 — The price cycle · 25s budget · 54 words (~22s spoken)

This is the credibility slide. Judges expect a lithium bull to wave the price away.

> "Prices tripled off the 2025 trough, then fell two hundred and seventy dollars a tonne
> in a single week in August as Bald Hill, Finniss and Jianxiawo restarted. We're not
> going to pretend that didn't happen. Wood Mackenzie still calls surplus into the early
> 2030s, and we carry their view as our bear case."

**Land this:** we carry the bear's view, not our own.

**Cut if behind:** the three mine names.

---

## Slide 5 — Structural demand · 22s budget · 50 words (~20s spoken)

> "Every team here will tell you lithium demand grows with electric vehicles. That
> argument rests on one policy variable and that variable just turned hostile. Ours
> doesn't need it. Storage installations grew about 51% last year, and storage is not
> subsidy-driven. The demand leg holds without a single EV assumption."

**Land this:** our demand case survives the subsidy repeal.

---

## Slide 6 — Cost curve · 35s budget · 86 words (~34s spoken)

Slow down. This is the mechanism that makes the bear case survivable.

> "Here's why we can be relaxed about the price. Our bear case assumes a realised price
> of about eleven hundred and forty-five US dollars a tonne. At that price the top quartile
> of world supply is under water and Pilgangoora is still generating cash margin.
>
> That's what swing supply means in practice. The tonnes that come off are somebody
> else's. PLS doesn't just survive a downturn, it buys assets during one. It bought
> Latin Resources in scrip at the trough and restarted Ngungaju when prices recovered."

**Land this:** at our bear price, a quarter of world supply loses money and PLS doesn't.

**Cut if behind:** the Latin Resources sentence.

> **Handover:** "That's the asset. Here's the argument."

# SPEAKER C — the shared-value case

## Slide 7 — Three levels · 12s budget · 24 words (~10s spoken)

You are explaining Porter and Kramer to the Shared Value Project. Do not teach them
their own framework.

> "Porter and Kramer describe three levels. Almost every mining ESG story lives at level
> two. We're claiming level one, and here's what that's worth."

**Cut if behind:** nothing. This is already the floor.

---

## Slide 8 — Carbon-declarable tonnes · 50s budget · 100 words (~40s spoken)

Walk the four boxes left to right as one chain. Each arrow is a "which means".

> "Calcination is the most energy-intensive step in the chain. PLS owns a Calix electric
> calciner outright, opened in June, that cuts those emissions by more than 80%. Which
> means the tonne has a low, *declarable* carbon footprint.
>
> That word is doing the work. From 2027 the EU Battery Regulation requires a carbon
> footprint declaration and a battery passport. US foreign-entity rules point the same
> way. A tonne that can't be declared can't enter those chains at any price.
>
> So this is a market-access story before it is a cost story. PLS is building tonnes that
> qualify where competitors' tonnes won't."

**Land this:** declarable. Say the word three times.

**Cut if behind:** the US foreign-entity clause.

---

## Slide 9 — The two cost levers · 50s budget · 107 words (~43s spoken)

> "Two levers, and we'll label them honestly.
>
> First, ore sorting. The A$103m P680 project installed a TOMRA sorter, over a thousand
> tonnes an hour. It rejects barren rock before crushing, so fewer tonnes get milled for
> the same lithium. FY26 unit costs fell fifty-eight dollars a tonne. We attribute about
> thirty of that to sorting.
>
> Second, power. Pilgangoora runs its own generation. Gas has displaced diesel, solar
> and storage are next. About eighteen dollars a tonne.
>
> Forty-eight dollars a tonne between them. Both are things a good operator would do
> without an ESG label, so we don't claim them as a differentiator. We claim them as
> cash."

**Land this:** A$48/t, and that we are not overclaiming it.

**Cut if behind:** the power detail. Say "power adds eighteen."

---

## Slide 10 — Where our story is thin · 30s budget · 71 words (~28s spoken)

Do not apologise and do not hedge. This is a scoring slide.

> "We went looking for holes in PLS's shared-value story and we found six. The net-zero
> target is a *decade*, not a year. The decade commencing 2040. There's no SBTi
> validation and no Scope 3 disclosure, on a company whose product is consumed
> downstream. The tailings method isn't disclosed either, so we carry it as an unpriced
> risk rather than assume it's fine.
>
> We took the haircut before we built the number."

**Land this:** a decade is not a date.

**Cut if behind:** the tailings sentence.

> **Handover:** "So here's the number after that haircut."

# SPEAKER D — valuation, risk and the close

## Slide 11 — The ESG value bridge · 62s budget · 126 words (~50s spoken)

The most important slide in the deck. Expect the double-counting question. Answer it
before it is asked.

> "First, what this bridge is not. Nothing on it is added to the DCF. Ore sorting and
> the power strategy are already inside the five sixty-nine cost base the model
> discounts. This is an attribution, not an addition. We're asking where the value comes
> from, not adding it twice.
>
> Forty-three cents a share. Thirty cents is the two cost levers. Thirteen cents is a
> P2000 permitting delay we think social licence avoids.
>
> Now the arithmetic that reframes the pitch. Measured against the six-fourteen target,
> forty-three cents is 4.6%. A rounding adjustment. Measured against the sixty-six cents
> of upside, it's 66%. Most of the investment case.
>
> And there's a further thirty-two cents of downstream value we've deliberately left
> *outside* the target. Nothing here depends on it landing."

**Land this:** attribution, not addition. Then 4.6% versus 66%.

**Cut if behind:** the thirty-two cents. Keep it for Q&A.

---

## Slide 12 — A finite orebody gets an annuity · 50s budget · 99 words (~40s spoken)

This separates you from every team that ran a textbook DCF.

> "Most DCFs end with a perpetuity growing at two and a half percent forever. A mine
> runs out.
>
> On our production profile Pilgangoora consumes 104 of its 334 million mineable tonnes
> by FY36 and exits at 14.3 million tonnes a year. About sixteen years left. So we value
> the terminal period as a sixteen-year annuity, not a perpetuity.
>
> That single choice is worth nine billion dollars of terminal value. A perpetuity would
> have handed us nineteen billion on an asset that runs out of rock. It's the difference
> between valuing a mine and valuing a company that never depletes."

**Land this:** A$9.0bn versus A$19.0bn.

**If asked whether the life is pre- or post-expansion:** post. Remaining ore divided by
the FY36 rate, which already includes P2000. Doubling throughput halves the life and
the model captures it.

## On the day

**Print the appendix index on one page and tape it next to the laptop.** Eighty-three
slides with no index is a liability. Fumbling for twenty seconds in Q&A costs more than
any slide you cut. Tab it by question, not by topic: *"double counting → E-bridge",
"why not a perpetuity → E12", "short interest → F2", "what you couldn't verify → F4".*

**Assign every Q&A section an owner before you walk in.** The failure mode is not being
wrong. It is four people looking at each other for three seconds.

**One person watches the clock and nobody else does.** If you are behind, that person
signals; the speaker takes the printed cut line and moves. Do not invent a cut live.

**If you are cut off at ten minutes**, the only thing you must have said is slide 11.
Everything else is recoverable in Q&A. That slide is not.

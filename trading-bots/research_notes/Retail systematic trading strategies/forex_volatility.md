## FX strategies (carry, momentum, value, trend, intraday)

### Takeaway
Carry, momentum and value in FX have modest published Sharpe ratios, and post-publication evidence shows decay. Momentum is cost-sensitive. Intraday time-of-day patterns (Breedon-Ranaldo) are documented in EBS data (1997-2007) but retail viability is unverified. No retail-spread data was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Currency momentum (Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling, Schrimpf, JFE 2012, 106(3):660-684; BIS WP 366, Dec 2011): cross-sectional spread between past winners and losers of up to 10% per year, distinct from carry, not strongly correlated with technical trading rules, not explained by traditional risk factors, partly explained by transaction costs, limits to arbitrage. — [EconPapers JFE](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:106:y:2012:i:3:p:660-684), [BIS WP366](https://www.bis.org/publ/work366.pdf)
- Momentum returns are "fairly sensitive to transaction costs"; adjusting for bid-ask spreads lowers profitability significantly (BIS snippet truncated). A Lisbon dissertation likewise concludes transaction costs are a plausible explanation for momentum but not for carry. — [BIS WP366](https://www.bis.org/publ/work366.pdf); [Portuguese Catholic Univ. dissertation](https://repositorio.ucp.pt/bitstreams/1b1db692-5b3f-4608-8064-290d78a8b83d/download)
- MSSS find momentum is driven by return continuation in spot rates, not interest differentials. — [Quantpedia currency momentum](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/currency-momentum-factor)
- Quantpedia FX carry trade: Sharpe 0.29, max drawdown -32.05%, based on Deutsche Bank Currency Carry index. Quantpedia lists Sharpe 0.3 on the currency momentum page (snippet unclear which strategy). — [Quantpedia FX carry](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/fx-carry-trade)
- Carry crash risk (Brunnermeier, Nagel, Pedersen, "Carry Trades and Currency Crashes", NBER WP 14473, 2008; NBER Macro Annual 2008): negative skewness from sudden unwinding when risk appetite and funding liquidity fall; rising VIX or TED associated with carry unwinding; funding liquidity predicts exchange rate moves. — [NBER w14473](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473); [Princeton slides](https://www.princeton.edu/~markus/research/papers/carry_trades_currency_crashes_slides_old.pdf)
- Trend following as hedge for carry risks: a Quantpedia-cited paper argues combined carry+trend outperforms either alone. — [Quantpedia new research on carry](https://quantpedia.com/new-research-paper-shows-how-to-easily-improve-5-fx-carry-trade/)
- Burnside and coauthors (2011): carry and momentum combinations held up out-of-sample and after transaction costs; Sharpe relative to equal-weighted carry rose from 0.57 to 0.86. — [search summary of Chicago/Burnside literature](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/658309) (that link is a Journal of Political Economy-style DOI page; the 0.57 to 0.86 claim was relayed by search summary, verify)
- Post-publication decay: Hutchinson et al. (2022, Int. Review of Financial Analysis, "Are carry, momentum and value still there in currencies?") report average out-of-sample Sharpe falling from +0.39 to -0.32, and currencies no longer responding to interest rate and real exchange rate differentials. — [UCC repository](https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/are-carry-momentum-and-value-still-there-in-currencies-2/)
- Technical rules study: average Sharpe of 388 strategy portfolios 0.62 in-sample, 0.06 out-of-sample (sample to April 2020); after modest costs excess returns are negative or insignificant. — [Univ. repository download](https://repository.adu.ac.ae/bitstreams/4912355c-88d1-434c-a489-e44e6ba6494b/download)
- Intraday (Breedon and Ranaldo, "Intraday Patterns in FX Returns and Order Flow", SNB WP 2011-04; QMUL WP 694, 2012; JMCB 2013, 45(5):953-965): currencies tend to depreciate during their own local trading hours; order flow shows market participants net buy foreign currency during local hours; data EBS spot and order flow 1997-2007; 2011 version states EUR/USD can form a simple profitable strategy. Exact hours, returns and costs not retrieved. — [SNB WP](https://www.snb.ch/en/publications/research/working-papers/2011/working_paper_2011_04); [QMUL](https://qmul.ac.uk/sef/research/workingpapers/2012/items/694.html); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jmoncb:v:45:y:2013:i:5:p:953-965)

### Inferences
- With the Hutchinson et al. decay and cost sensitivity, a retail FX factor bot should expect Sharpe near 0 to 0.4 gross on majors, and likely lower net of retail spreads; momentum on majors only (4 pairs) is far narrower than the paper's cross-section.
- The Breedon-Ranaldo effect (e.g., EUR/USD weakening during European hours, USD strengthening during US hours) is testable on Dukascopy hourly data but its edge may be smaller than retail spread plus swap; backtest with spread of ~0.5-1.5 pips needed.
- Carry crash risk can be handled by a VIX filter (carry off when VIX high or rising), consistent with BNP findings, but this is an inference, not tested evidence.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: LRV (Lustig, Roussanov, Verdelhan) "Common Risk Factors in Currency Markets" / dollar factor numbers; Burnside "Do peso problems explain carry" details; FX value/PPP numbers; trend-following Sharpe on G10 FX (e.g., Olszewski-Zhou, Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen); London fix and Asian range breakout evidence; retail spread/swap costs (Babypips, Dukascopy); post-2020 out-of-sample for any FX factor through 2026.
- Retail data note [UNVERIFIED-MEMORY]: Yahoo EURUSD=X data is indicative quotes; carry needs swap/rollover rates not in Yahoo data.

## Volatility premium (VIX futures roll, short-vol ETPs, term-structure filters, Cooper)

### Takeaway
Short VIX futures in contango earns a documented risk premium (Simon-Campasano 2006-2011) but ETP-based implementations have very large drawdowns; Volmageddon (Feb 2018) destroyed XIV and forced SVXY to 0.5x leverage. The newest practitioner/academic evidence (Zarattini-Mele-Aziz 2025) reports about 16.3% CAGR and Sharpe ~1 using VIX/VIX3M and VRP signals, but independent replications are weaker and parameter-sensitive.

### Cited Findings
- Simon and Campasano, "The VIX Futures Basis: Evidence and Trading Strategies" (2012 WP; EFMA 2013): basis does not forecast spot VIX change but forecasts VIX futures returns; short futures in contango and long in backwardation was profitable; a hedged strategy (E-mini S&P hedge) reportedly 53% annual compound return 2006-2011 (secondary summary; treat with caution); authors interpret basis as a harvestable risk premium. Exact thresholds not retrieved. — [EFMA 2013 paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2013-Reading/papers/EFMA2013_0164_fullpaper.pdf); summary via [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/portfolio-strategies-for-volatility-investing/)
- Zarattini, Aziz, Mele, "The Volatility Edge: A Dual Approach for VIX ETNs Trading" (SFI WP 25-91, Oct 2025): volatility risk premium portfolios from VIX-linked ETNs only; 2008-2025 after realistic costs, 16.3% annualized, Sharpe about 1, equity correlation about 15%; signals are expected VRP and VIX vs VIX3M term structure; small allocation raises blended portfolio Sharpe about 20%. — [SFI](https://sfi.ch/en/publications/n-25-91-the-volatility-edge-a-dual-approach-for-vix-etns-trading); [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/the-volatility-edge-a-dual-approach-for-vix-etns-trading/)
- Rule as implemented in a QuantConnect port: short vol when VIX below VIX3M, long vol when above, size tied to expected VRP. An independent QuantConnect backtest from January 2016 shows Sharpe 0.729 vs 0.582 for SPY buy-and-hold. A forum reviewer found the vixRatio threshold not robust (1% change cut returns by over 35%). — [QuantConnect research](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/21143/harvesting-the-volatility-risk-premium-with-a-dual-vix-signal/p1); [QC forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/7984)
- Practitioner rule: if ^VIX > ^VIX3M at close, do not open VIXY/SVXY/SVIX positions and close existing ones; short evidence window only. — [layquant Substack](https://layquant.substack.com/p/i-call-it-volatility-reversion)
- Other QuantConnect VIX-ETN backtests range from CAGR 16.35% / drawdown 17.8% to drawdown 65.5%; SVXY splits (two during 2017-19) cause data gaps if not adjusted. — [QC backtest](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_0dbf858d3b11cf9d587826808b76bf23.html)
- Static short-vol via ETPs: profitable long-term but small Sharpe (<0.5) due to high VIX volatility. — [Journal of Investment Strategies](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-investment-strategies/5460971/statistics-of-vix-futures-and-their-applications-to-trading-volatility-exchange-traded-products)
- Cooper, "Easy Volatility Investing" (Feb 2013 SSRN): XIV average annualized return 31.4% with very large drawdowns; claims a 10% volatility allocation in a bond portfolio lifts Sharpe from 1 to 2.2 (own analysis, pre-2018, simulated pre-inception data). — [paper copy](https://trevcan.duckdns.org/files/trevor/papers/emintham-Papers/Cooper-%20Easy%20Volatility%20Investing.pdf)
- Independent checks of Cooper-type rules: CXO replication found long-VXX construct annual Sharpe -0.77, VXZ construct -0.16 over whole sample; a QuantConnect "Strategy 3" run showed Sharpe 0.393 with 65.5% drawdown; blog replications of a moving-average rule report Sharpe 1.52 and 2.49 without costs. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/volatility-trading-strategies/); [QC backtest](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_e305a07e3d0cd4ac68d862d1bfd97e7d.html); [systematicinvestor](https://systematicinvestor.github.io/Volatility-Strategy); [r-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/volatility-risk-premium-sharpe-2-return-to-drawdown-3/)
- Volmageddon: on 5 Feb 2018 short-vol ETPs lost over 90% (BIS: XIV down 84%); Credit Suisse terminated XIV (last trade 20 Feb 2018; trigger described as loss of 80%+ of indicative value); ProShares cut SVXY from -1x to -0.5x effective 28 Feb 2018 and also reduced UVXY leverage. — [BIS Quarterly Review Mar 2018](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1803t.htm); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/volatility-spike-triggers-inverse-etf-xiv-closure-2018-02-07); [CFA Institute FAJ 2021](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2021/volmageddon-failure-short-volatility-products); [Bloomberg Quint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/markets/2018/02/27/proshares-slashes-leverage-on-surviving-volatility-products)

### Inferences
- Current SVXY (0.5x, post-Feb 2018 history) has structurally lower tail risk than pre-2018 SVXY/XIV; backtests using Yahoo SVXY pre-2018 are 1x and are not comparable to live product. Need to use split-adjusted data and to account for leverage change.
- VXX/UVXY long-only is a decaying instrument (CXO Sharpe -0.77); only usable as a short-lived hedge or with a strict VIX/VIX3M backwardation filter.
- The VIX/VIX3M<1 filter (contango) is the best-supported retail rule (Zarattini et al., practitioner posts), but its robustness is contested; use a threshold range and walk-forward testing, and size small given Volmageddon-type gaps.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: Carr-Wu "Variance risk premiums", Bakshi-Kapadia, Whaley VIX papers; Volatility Made Simple and SixFigureInvesting specific rule/performance tables (search did not return them); exact Zarattini rules/max drawdown; independent post-2018 and 2022-2026 out-of-sample results; behavior in April 2025 volatility spike [UNVERIFIED-MEMORY, not searched]; VIX spike buy-signal and equity timing evidence.

## Gold, commodities, bonds simple rules

### Takeaway
Treasury turn-of-month / month-end effects have a plausible flow-based mechanism and practitioner support but contested ETF-level evidence; gold seasonality evidence is weak.

### Cited Findings
- Practitioners (credited to Kris Longmore, Robot Wealth) report TLT weakens early month and rallies in last days of month; backtests find TLT gains concentrated after seventh trading day or in the final ~7 trading days (window-dressing explanation is speculative). Methodology and returns not verified. — [Beyond Passive](https://beyondpassive.substack.com/p/two-calendar-effects-at-the-month); [Quantified Strategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/long-term-treasuries-calendar-effects)
- NY Fed Liberty Street: Treasury note and bond activity concentrates on last trading day of month, likely from passive funds' turn-of-month rebalancing. — [Liberty Street Economics 2024](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2024/09/end-of-month-liquidity-in-the-treasury-market/)
- Contrary: CXO Advisory review of turn-of-month across asset classes reports TLT results since mid-2002 do not corroborate the paper's results. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/does-the-turn-of-the-month-effect-work-for-asset-classes)
- Gold: Seasonax-based Interactive Brokers analysis (1998-2023) finds a turn-of-month effect exists but is not strong; not peer-reviewed. — [IBKR Campus](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/commodities/how-does-gold-perform-around-the-turn-of-the-month/)

### Inferences
- A TLT end-of-month long rule is cheap to backtest on Yahoo daily (TLT total return, dividends paid monthly near start of month matter) but should be validated post-2022 given rate regime change; treat as low-confidence.

### Gaps
- Lou, Polk, Skouras "A tug of war: overnight versus intraday expected returns" and the Treasury auction-effect papers were not found; no verified gold seasonality numbers; no net-of-cost results; no 2024-2026 out-of-sample data.

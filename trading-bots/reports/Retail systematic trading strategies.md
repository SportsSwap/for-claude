# Slow trend and dip rules beat indicators

**The strategies with the best evidence of surviving publication and costs are slow, diversified trend and momentum rules, short-horizon index dip-buying, and one carefully sized volatility-premium trade. Popular YouTube indicator systems are the weakest.** Century-scale studies put multi-asset trend following at a net Sharpe of about 0.7-0.8 ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing)), though only as a backtest by a firm that sells the product, and the 2010s were far weaker. Faber's 10-month moving-average rule roughly halved drawdowns historically (Faber, SSRN 962461, via [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/asset-class-trend-following)). Most listed equity anomalies keep some of their return after publication, but McLean-Pontiff find roughly 58% decay ([Econpapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:71:y:2016:i:1:p:5-32)), and currency carry, momentum and value turned negative out of sample ([Hutchinson et al.](https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/are-carry-momentum-and-value-still-there-in-currencies-2/)). Viral MACD/200-EMA systems advertised at an 86% win rate won about 31% in long samples and lost after costs ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780027)). Section 2 sets out ten bots in three confidence tiers, with exact rules. Section 3 lists what to skip. Section 4 sets the pass/fail standards. **Important caveat: every figure here comes from web-search result summaries.** Full-page and PDF reads were blocked by the environment's network policy (arxiv, SSRN, NBER, AQR, CXO and most others failed DNS or allowlist). Treat every number as a lead to verify against the primary paper, and formulas marked "from memory" in the source notes must be checked before being hard-coded. Parameters in my rule sets are marked as starting points where the sources did not confirm them.

## What the evidence says survives publication and costs

Three findings shape everything that follows. First, **the broad factor literature disagrees mostly because of method, not facts.** Hou, Xue and Zhang find that with microcaps mitigated, 65% of 452 anomalies fail the |t| >= 1.96 test and 82% fail a multiple-testing hurdle of 2.78 ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/23394)). Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen report an 82.4% Bayesian replication rate across 153 factors ([NBER](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)), and Chen and Zimmermann reproduce 98% of 180 predictors ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3604626)). A retail trader confined to liquid ETFs and large caps should lean towards the pessimistic end, because the gap is mostly value-weighting and microcap treatment. Second, **the cleanest evidence is for slow signals on diversified liquid markets.** Lempérière et al. report trend t-stats of about 5 for futures since 1960 and about 10 back to 1800, and note that trend predictability saturates for large signals, which argues for capped signals ([arXiv 1404.3274](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1404.3274)). Baltas and Kosowski find no significant evidence of capacity constraints on trend funds, and only rising cross-asset correlation explains the post-2008 weakness ([EDHEC](https://climateinstitute.edhec.edu/publications/momentum-strategies-futures-markets-and-trend-following-funds)). Huang et al. add a real challenge: asset-by-asset time-series momentum predictability is weak, and the strategy performs about the same as one based on the historical sample mean ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2020/03/an-empirical-challenge-for-trend-following/)). The practical reading is that trend rules work mainly as drawdown control and crisis diversification, so judge them by drawdown and decades, not by raw Sharpe.

Third, **regime variance is large.** The SG CTA Index returned +20.13% in 2022 ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/flat-december-sees-ctas-complete-record-year-2022/)), then the SG Trend Index fell 9.3% through April 2025 ([Alternatives Watch](https://www.alternativeswatch.com/2025/06/02/trend-following-hedge-funds-endure-more-pain-societe-generale-indices/)) and finished 2025 at about +2.39% ([TheFullFX](https://thefullfx.com/ctas-end-2025-on-a-positive-note/)). Third-party recaps conflict on some 2026 figures, so I do not rely on them.

What retail cannot copy is equally informative. Medallion's reported 66% gross and 39% net (1988-2018) comes from short-horizon signals needing tick data, financing and capacity ([Wikipedia summary of Zuckerman](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Medallion_Fund)). Jane Street's 2024 net trading revenue of $20.5B comes largely from ETF-versus-basket arbitrage and market making ([Global Trading](https://www.globaltrading.net/?p=38181)). Daily-bar pairs trading has decayed: Do and Faff show top-pair excess returns falling from 0.86%/month (1962-88) to 0.24% (2003-09) ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v66y2010i4p83-95.html)). Machine learning shows the same pattern. Fischer and Krauss's LSTM reported a 5.8 gross Sharpe, but profits were about zero after costs from 2010 ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ejores:v:270:y:2018:i:2:p:654-669)). Avramov et al. show ML profits concentrate in hard-to-arbitrage stocks and shrink sharply under value-weighting ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:69:y:2023:i:5:p:2587-2619)). The notes found no evidence on D.E. Shaw, Two Sigma, Man AHL or Winton methods, so the "hedge fund practice" evidence base here is essentially AQR trend research, Renaissance and Jane Street anecdotes, and CTA index returns.

## Ten bots to build, ranked by evidence quality

The first four are the core. The next four have decent evidence with material caveats. The last two are low-confidence experiments and should get the smallest research budget. Execution convention for all daily bots: signal on the close, fill at the next open, total-return (adjusted) prices, and costs applied. Why this ranking. Bots 1-4 are slow, low-turnover, and rest on the broadest evidence, so costs barely matter and the main risk is regime drift. Bots 6-7 have high stated hit rates but the post-2016 evidence is mixed, so they earn a place as a diversifying, low-exposure sleeve rather than a core holding. Bot 5 has the best recent academic headline but the worst tail, so it belongs at small size behind a hard exit rule. Bot 9 sits low because its own literature documents decay. Bot 10 exists mainly to test whether cheap calendar effects survive in 2022-2026 data. Several bots are natural complements: trend rules (1, 2, 4) pair with dip-buying (6, 7), which is long-biased when trend rules are in cash. Carry paired with trend improves on either alone in one cited study ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/new-research-paper-shows-how-to-easily-improve-5-fx-carry-trade/)). No sourced correlation matrix exists, so measure sleeve correlations yourself before allocating.

Volatility targeting belongs inside bot 2 as a sizing rule, not as a separate bot. Moreira-Muir report a 4.9% alpha and a 25% higher Sharpe ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208.pdf)), but Cederburg et al. find real-time versions across 103 strategies generally do not beat unmanaged ones ([Arizona](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/on-the-performance-of-volatility-managed-portfolios/)), and the original weights contain look-ahead. Use only lagged volatility, cap leverage, and count turnover. For sizing overall, use fractional Kelly of a shrunk edge estimate: full Kelly implies roughly a 50% chance of a 50% drawdown in the idealised model ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion)).

## What has decayed, been debunked, or cannot be run from free data

Most popular indicator strategies fail the moment they get a long sample. The viral "MACD cross below zero plus 200 EMA" claimed 86% wins but scored 31.4% on 16+ years of gold M30 data, lost after costs and was positive in only 2 of 16 years ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/780027)). A 27-year @ES review found about 32% wins and losses in the last five years ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/BTCUSD/FbqjaZja-QUIT-RELYING-ON-AUTOMATED-BACK-TESTING/?amp)). On USD/JPY 4h over 10 years the same system returned -22.1% (CAGR -2.5%) against +55.5% for buy-and-hold ([Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/macd-200-ema-usd-jpy)). In FX, 388 technical-rule portfolios averaged an in-sample Sharpe of 0.62 and an out-of-sample 0.06 (sample to April 2020), with negative or insignificant excess returns after modest costs ([repository](https://repository.adu.ac.ae/bitstreams/4912355c-88d1-434c-a489-e44e6ba6494b/download)). Sullivan-Timmermann-White applied a reality-check bootstrap to the technical-rule universe on 100 years of DJIA data ([LSE](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119144)). The notes recall that apparent profits did not survive the adjustment, but that conclusion was not confirmed in this session, so verify it. For UT Bot, Hull Suite, Ichimoku and Squeeze Momentum, the notes found no credible large-sample tests either way, so treat them as unproven rather than refuted. The one partial exception is Supertrend: a weekly S&P 500 test back to 1960 says it can add value as a trend filter ([QS](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/the-supertrend-indicator-backtested)), which fits the general pattern that slow filters work and fast signal indicators do not.

Other things to skip. Buy-at-close/sell-at-open on SPY looks strong in raw data (717% overnight vs 627% buy-and-hold, 1993-Jan 2020), but trading costs wipe it out ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/trading-costs-wipe-out-the-overnight-return-anomaly/)), and QuantConnect versions range from Sharpe 0.22 to -0.11. Long VXX or UVXY is a decaying instrument: a CXO replication found a Sharpe of -0.77 for a long-VXX construct, and Cooper's "Easy Volatility Investing" (XIV average return 31.4%, simulated pre-inception data) did not hold up in independent checks ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/volatility-trading-strategies/)). Crypto funding and basis carry has compressed (futures yield "once over 20%, now less than Treasury notes" per [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/the-bitcoin-futures-yield-collapse-once-over-20-now-less-than-treasury-notes-202608031149)), needs margin on two venues, and cannot be tested from Yahoo OHLC. On-chain and halving-cycle signals are weakening, with MVRV peaks falling from 5.88 to 2.74 across epochs per a non-peer-reviewed preprint ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26188)), and four cycles is not a statistical sample. Cross-sectional crypto momentum is sample-dependent, crash-prone (max drawdown 55% in one 2026 study) and inflated by survivorship when built from today's top coins ([Journals VU](https://www.journals.vu.lt/BATP/lt/article/download/44540/42590/138419), [Vaasa](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/bitstreams/2a766d58-9fd3-44b8-b1a3-14a048a0b653/download)). End-of-day leveraged-ETF and options-gamma flow effects need intraday data ([Fed FEDS 2013-48](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2013/201348/201348pap.pdf)). The Breedon-Ranaldo FX intraday pattern was measured on 1997-2007 EBS data, and its edge may be smaller than a retail spread ([SNB](https://www.snb.ch/en/publications/research/working-papers/2011/working_paper_2011_04)), so test it only as a side experiment on Dukascopy hourly data. Short-term crypto time-of-day edges are mostly vendor backtests, and BTC's US-hours share of realised variance has risen to about 50.6% since late 2021 ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/crypto-never-closes-but-bitcoin-ethereum-xrp-and-solana-now-move-on-wall-street-time/)). Treat any pre-2021 hour-of-day result as stale.

## Backtesting standards every bot must pass

The core principle is that the number of variants tried is part of the result. Count every parameter combination, every bot and every discarded idea as a trial. Harvey, Liu and Zhu argue a new discovery needs a t-ratio above 3.0 given how many factors have been tested ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). Harvey and Liu's haircut Sharpe converts the Sharpe to a t-stat, adjusts the p-value for the number of tests (Bonferroni, Holm or BHY) and converts back, with marginal Sharpes penalised most ([Harvey-Liu](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)). The Deflated Sharpe Ratio of Bailey and Lopez de Prado is the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio evaluated against the expected maximum Sharpe of N skill-less trials, adjusting for skew and kurtosis. In implementation, use per-period rather than annualised Sharpe or the statistic saturates ([fynance docs](https://fynance.readthedocs.io/en/v2.10.1/generated/fynance.research.deflated_sharpe_ratio.html)). The Probability of Backtest Overfitting uses combinatorially symmetric cross-validation: split the trial-by-time return matrix into S blocks, take every half-split as in-sample and out-of-sample, pick the in-sample winner, and count how often it ranks below the out-of-sample median ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-computational-finance/2471206/the-probability-of-backtest-overfitting)). The DSR and CSCV formulas in the source notes are recalled from memory and must be checked against the papers before coding.

Validation should combine several gates. Use walk-forward (Pardo) with walk-forward efficiency, that is annualised OOS return over annualised IS return, of at least 50%, and at least half of windows profitable, with no single window above 50% of net profit ([TradeStation](https://help.tradestation.com/10_00/eng/tswfo/topics/walkforward_test_results.htm), [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/walk-forward-efficiency-ratio-thresholds)). Add purged, embargoed combinatorial cross-validation for any model with overlapping labels (six groups with two as test gives 15 splits and 5 paths) ([skfolio](https://skfolio.org/generated/skfolio.model_selection.CombinatorialPurgedCV.html)). Follow Kevin Davey's process of one-shot walk-forward, Monte Carlo resampling of trades, then 6-12 months of paper incubation, with no edits after a failed gate ([Algo Advantage](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about)). Size so that worst-case Monte Carlo drawdown fits your personal limit. My suggested acceptance bar, which is convention rather than a sourced standard, is net-of-cost OOS Sharpe of about 0.7 or higher (lower, around 0.5, is acceptable for the slow TAA sleeves where drawdown is the product), Calmar at least 0.5, profit factor at least 1.3, DSR at least 0.95, PBO below about 0.3, a parameter plateau (neighbouring values keep at least 50-70% of base Sharpe), survival under 2x cost stress, and a t-stat above 3 when many variants were searched. Trade count matters: the 100-trade minimum is only blog-level guidance, and low-frequency bots (monthly TAA, Oops-type patterns at about 7 trades a year on one index per [Unger Academy](https://ungeracademy.com/?p=7737)) cannot satisfy it, so judge them on portfolio-level, multi-market results and long histories instead.

Costs and data hygiene. These cost figures are my conservative suggestions, not sourced results: 1-3 bps per side for liquid US ETFs; for crypto 10 bps fee plus 5-10 bps slippage per side (60 bps on a Coinbase-style retail tier); roughly 1 pip round trip for FX majors plus swap. Reference points from the notes: IBKR Pro tiered commissions run $0.0005-$0.0035 per share with a $1.00 minimum ([IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/commissions)), EURUSD retail spreads average about 0.59 pips across 60 brokers (range 0.0-3.0) ([BrokerChooser](https://brokerchooser.com/broker-costs/spread-comparison-in-germany)), and Binance base spot tier is 0.10% per side while Coinbase Advanced is commonly cited at 0.4% maker/0.6% taker, which may be out of date ([Coinbase](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/trading-and-funding/advanced-trade/advanced-trade-fees)). Never fill at the signal bar's close, handle the SVXY leverage change and splits, and use dividend-adjusted prices. Yahoo's EURUSD indicative quotes are not a substitute for Dukascopy data, and carry needs rate data Yahoo does not supply. I did not verify Australian-specific items (IBKR Australia product access, tax treatment of ETFs, crypto and CFDs, AUD conversion costs); confirm those before committing capital.

## Conclusion

The evidence says build fewer, slower, cheaper bots than a retail trader's instinct suggests. The ranked bots are all explicit about what they buy: drawdown control (Faber, trend, BTC filter), a low-exposure short-term edge (RSI(2), IBS) or a small tail-risk-bearing premium (VIX term structure). Almost none of them is a pure Sharpe-maximiser once decay is accounted for, and the published headline numbers are likely to be cut roughly in half or worse in live use. The key out-of-sample test is whether rules with parameters frozen before 2022 survive 2022-2026, a period that contains a bear market, a record CTA year and a poor first half of 2025.

Two research gaps could change the ranking, and both are cheap to close once network access is available. The notes never retrieved independent post-2013 results for GEM or the Keller family, an independently costed 2015-2026 SPY test of the Connors rules, or any verified post-2010 net-of-cost numbers for calendar effects. And the strongest recent claim, the 16.3% / Sharpe-1 VIX term-structure result, is a single new working paper whose threshold sensitivity has already been challenged. Verify primary sources first for bots 5 and 6, since they carry the largest gap between headline and plausible live results.

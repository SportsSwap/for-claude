# Thesis architecture — restructured after council review

## What was wrong with v1

The council's First Principles and Expansionist advisors independently landed on the same
critique, and it is correct:

> Two of three pillars (ore sorting, power intensity) are pure cost/efficiency plays — you'd
> fund TOMRA sorters and cheaper power regardless of ESG framing. Calling that "shared value"
> is relabeling capex discipline. Pillar three (social licence protecting permitting) is risk
> avoidance, not value creation. Meanwhile the two moves that actually resemble Porter-Kramer
> shared value — Calix midstream and the POSCO JV — get valued at zero. They found the real
> thesis and declined to price it.

The arithmetic condemned it: a bridge worth A$0.43/share on a A$6.12 target is 4.6%. By our own
numbers ESG was a rounding adjustment on a lithium-price-recovery stock.

## Porter & Kramer's three levels — and where v1 sat

| Level | What it means | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Reconceiving products and markets** | Serve an unmet societal need in a way that opens a new market | absent | **the thesis** |
| **2. Redefining value-chain productivity** | Solve an environmental problem in a way that lowers cost | all of it | supporting |
| **3. Enabling local cluster development** | Build the surrounding ecosystem you depend on | absent | supporting |

v1 lived almost entirely at level 2 — the weakest, most commoditised level, and the one every
other team will also reach for.

## v2 — the restructured argument

### Level 1: the carbon intensity of the tonne is becoming a market-access test, not a cost line

This is the thesis. PLS is building the ability to sell a **low-carbon, traceable, non-China
lithium unit** rather than a commodity rock:

- **Calix mid-stream plant.** 100% owned, opened 5-Jun-2026, first production guided Sep-qtr
  2026. Electric calcination cuts emissions from calcination — the most energy-intensive step
  in the chain — by more than 80%. It also moves PLS from selling concentrate to selling
  lithium phosphate, capturing conversion margin.
- **POSCO JV.** 18% of a 43ktpa lithium hydroxide plant at Gwangyang, with an option to 30%.
  A Korean, non-China conversion route into Japanese and Korean cell makers.
- **Why it is a market, not a saving.** The EU Battery Regulation requires a carbon footprint
  declaration and a battery passport; US FEOC rules restrict Chinese-linked supply chains;
  Korean and Japanese OEMs are qualifying suppliers on provenance. Carbon intensity and
  provenance are becoming *qualification criteria*. A tonne that cannot be declared cannot
  be sold into the highest-value chains at any price.

### Level 2: productivity — real, but named honestly for what it is

Ore sorting and the power strategy do lower cost and emissions together. We keep them, we
quantify them, and we say plainly that these are efficiency gains a good operator would pursue
regardless of the ESG label. Claiming otherwise is what a judge would puncture.

### Level 3: cluster — the part that makes the growth executable

Traditional Owner agreements, Pilbara local content, and in Brazil the Salinas / Vale do
Jequitinhonha development (including the flour-factory revitalisation, ~300 direct and indirect
jobs) build the labour and supplier base that P2000 and Colina need in order to be built at all.

## The contradiction we must resolve, not bury

v1 argued social licence protects the P2000 schedule while simultaneously reporting that the
Pilbara Environmental Offsets Fund PLS pays into was independently found underfunded
(A$893–3,781/ha, ABC review, Oct-2024). That is the thesis undermining itself.

**Resolution:** the offsets *system* is inadequate — a sector-wide exposure, not a PLS failure.
That makes it a live risk to every Pilbara miner's licence, and an opportunity for the operator
that gets ahead of it. It becomes one of our engagement asks of management, not a footnote.

## The comparative question the case actually poses

Six stocks were offered. The differentiator has to be comparative — whose mechanism creates
more durable advantage — not PLS analysed alone.

| | How ESG works in the business | What it does to value |
|---|---|---|
| BHP, Rio Tinto, Fortescue, South32 | Scope 1 and 2 abatement on iron ore, coal, alumina. ESG spend defends an existing licence and an existing cash flow. | **Defensive.** Cost of staying in business. |
| Mineral Resources | Also lithium, but geared (net debt ~US$4.3bn, 1.7x EBITDA), a conglomerate with mining services, and governance controversies. | Diluted, and balance sheet constrains optionality. |
| **PLS Group** | The societal need *is* the demand driver, and the carbon intensity of the unit determines which market it can be sold into. | **Offensive.** ESG creates the market. |

For a *shared value* mandate specifically, only one of the six has ESG on the revenue line
rather than the cost line.

## What this changes in the deliverables

1. Rebuild the ESG value bridge around three tiers: In the base case (productivity levers),
   Not in the base case but sized (the low-carbon downstream option), and Explicitly unpriced.
2. Size the downstream option properly instead of carrying it at zero, and keep it outside the
   target price so the target stays conservative.
3. Add a comparative slide answering "why PLS over the other five" on shared-value grounds.
4. Lead the ESG section with the mechanism chain, not the bridge:
   ore sorting and electric calcination → lower carbon intensity per tonne → qualification for
   EU/Korean/Japanese chains → offtake security and margin → funded growth option.
5. Use the Q4 FY26 realised price of US$2,107/t as the near-term catalyst: the September-quarter
   print lands inside the investment horizon.
6. Appendix: combinatorial upside — P2000 x Colina x POSCO-to-30% x mid-stream.
